AI Isn't Replacing L&D — But L&D Professionals Who Use AI Well Will Have an Advantage

The real question isn't whether to use AI. It's whether we learn to use it thoughtfully.

Let's start with something clear: AI won't replace the human skills that make great learning possible. Empathy, curiosity, the ability to read a room, to ask the right question at the right time — these aren't going anywhere.

But AI is already changing how we research, design, personalise and deliver learning. And the L&D professionals who learn to use it well? They're going to have a real advantage.

What AI Actually Does Well

I've been experimenting with AI tools in my own L&D work for the past year. Here's what I've found it genuinely useful for:

  • Research acceleration: Getting up to speed on new topics, finding relevant frameworks, identifying key questions to explore.
  • Draft generation: Creating first drafts of learning materials, quiz questions, scenario outlines — things I then refine with actual expertise.
  • Personalisation at scale: Adapting content for different audiences, creating variations for different experience levels.
  • Administrative reduction: Summarising feedback, organising notes, generating structured outlines from messy thinking.

What AI Can't Do (And Won't Soon)

Here's the other side. AI struggles with:

  • Understanding organisational context: The politics, the history, the unwritten rules that determine whether learning actually sticks.
  • Reading the room: Knowing when a group needs a break, when to push harder, when to change approach entirely.
  • Designing for behaviour change: Real behaviour change requires understanding motivation, resistance, incentives — deeply human things.
  • Building trust: People learn from people they trust. AI doesn't build trust.

"The tool doesn't replace the craft. It changes what the craft looks like."

The Real Question

The question isn't "Should L&D professionals use AI?" That ship has sailed.

The question is: How do we use AI thoughtfully, without losing the human side of learning?

Here's my working framework:

1. Use AI for acceleration, not replacement

Let AI handle the first draft, the research legwork, the administrative overhead. Then apply your actual expertise — your understanding of your learners, your organisation, your subject matter.

2. Stay curious about what it's doing

Don't just accept AI output. Question it. Test it. Understand where it's strong and where it's weak. The more you understand the tool, the better you can use it.

3. Protect the human elements

The conversations, the facilitation, the coaching, the relationship-building — these are where real learning happens. Don't let efficiency gains here actually reduce effectiveness.

4. Experiment publicly

Share what you're learning. What works? What doesn't? The L&D community is figuring this out together, and we'll get there faster if we're honest about both successes and failures.

Key Takeaway

AI is a tool, not a replacement. The L&D professionals who thrive will be those who use AI to amplify their human expertise — not those who try to let AI replace it.

One Practical Experiment

Here's something to try this week:

Take a learning design task you're working on. Use AI to generate a first draft — whether that's learning objectives, a session outline, quiz questions, or scenario ideas. Then, critically review it. What's useful? What's missing? What needs to change based on your actual context?

Notice where AI added value. Notice where your expertise was essential. That's the sweet spot.

Until then, stay curious.
